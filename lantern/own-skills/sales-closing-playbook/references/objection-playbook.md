# Objection playbook (paraphrased from the source videos)

Method for every objection: **empathise** ("I get that"), **curious tilt** (ask what's behind it), **isolate** ("is that the only thing?"), **resolve**, **ask again**. Never argue. A comment ("that's expensive") is not a "no".

Objections come in three layers: **circumstances** (time, money, fit) -> **other people** (partner, boss) -> **self** (doubt, fear). People blame the outer layer first. "I need to think about it" is usually two layers in, which is progress.

## "I need to think about it"
- "Of course. What are the main things you're weighing?" then "What are you most afraid will happen?" then "What would make it a yes? A no?"
- Play it out: what does "thinking about it" look like in a month? Another stretch of the same situation?
- Best case / worst case: spell out the worst case (often what they have now) and the best case; "Is that a bet you'd take?"
- "You'll probably do it eventually, why not start now?"

## Price / "too expensive" / "can't afford"
- "Is it a lot of money to you?" (If yes, good: skin in the game.) "Compared to the result you want, is it worth it?" If yes, it's about *belief or access*, not price.
- Resourcefulness not resources: people find money for emergencies or things they truly want.
- Never lower the price. Change terms: prepay discount -> half down -> financing -> instalments -> a smaller starter step. "Everyone buys something."
- Cheap competitor: "If ours cost the same, which would you pick and why?" Let them list your strengths.

## "Not a good time / too busy"
- "Will you be busy again later? Then it has to work *while* you're busy; that's when support matters."
- Separate priority from time. Subtract tasks rather than adding. Ask when they'd pictured starting.

## "I need to talk to my partner / boss"
- Best: get them on the call early ("who else is involved?").
- Otherwise: "What do you think they'd push back on?" Work those points.
- "What would happen if they said no?" (If they'd go ahead anyway, the partner was a smokescreen.)
- "Do they know you're dealing with this? If they dislike the problem, why dislike the fix?"
- Support vs permission: "Do you need their approval, or their support?" Use a short guarantee or small first step if suitable.
- Large B2B: bring more stakeholders in; relationship building beats closing tricks.

## "Not sure it's for me" / "I've been burned before"
- "If this were perfect, would you do it? What's missing?" Fix small dislikes with a swap.
- "What happened last time?" then: a bad past purchase shouldn't make you miss a good one.
- Identity framing: new goals need new priorities.

## "Too fast" / "send me info" / "I'll try free content first"
- Not fast if they've wanted this for years. Info requests: "Happy to. What specifically would you want to see so I send the right thing?" and book the next step.
- Free content: "Do that now. Either it works, or you hit the next problem and we can help. Either way, start."

## "No guarantee?"
"I control my part, not yours. Are you ready to do your part?" Offer the guarantee you can honestly stand behind.

## Asking again (looping)
"If we can solve that, do you want to move forward?" Repeat as many times as you resolve a *real* concern each time. Re-asking with nothing new annoys people.
