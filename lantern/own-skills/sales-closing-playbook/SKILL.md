# Sales Closing Playbook

Method summarised in original wording from public sales-training videos (list in `references/sources.md`). It is a personal working playbook, not a script to read out. The user is often a beginner: explain in plain words, give ready-to-use lines, ask one question at a time.

## First, find out the mode
Ask (briefly) what they sell, to whom, price range, and where deals die. Then pick:
1. **Write a call plan/script** -> follow "The call, step by step".
2. **Answer an objection** -> `references/objection-playbook.md` + the 3A reframe below.
3. **Role-play drill** -> you play the prospect; see "Drill mode".
4. **Pre-call prep** -> prep card below.
5. **Funnel / pipeline review** -> `references/pipeline-and-metrics.md`.
6. **Follow-up messages** -> see "Follow-up".

## Core beliefs (apply everywhere)
- Sales = raising the odds the person buys. Improve every stage, not only the close.
- Ask, don't tell. People believe what *they* say, not what you say. Whoever answers questions gets interrogated; keep the prospect talking (aim ~2:1 listening to talking).
- Nobody can disagree with a question. Never argue; if you win the argument you lose the sale.
- Be a curious guide, not a hero: understand first, then make them right, not you.
- Speed and availability beat polish: fast response, same-day slots, a booked next step every time.
- Only sell to people it genuinely helps. Be truly fine with "no". Never invent scarcity, fake colleagues, fake testimonials or false claims.

## The call, step by step
1. **Prep (about 10% of call length; 5 min is plenty).** Prep card: who they are, what they said/booked for, anything from earlier notes, one specific thing to reference. Open by repeating earlier notes so they never repeat themselves.
2. **Open with an agenda (Proof, Promise, Plan, ~20 seconds).** "You booked about [goal]. We help people like you do [result]. Here's how this goes: I ask a few questions, we see if it's a fit, and either way you leave with something useful."
3. **CLOSER**
   - **C**larify: why they are here *today*? "What made you take this call?" then "why is that important?"
   - **L**abel: restate it: "So you're here, want to get there, and X is in the way. Right?"
   - **O**verview past pain: what they tried, what it cost (time, money, stress), what staying stuck costs per week. What they tried before is your edge: it didn't work, or they'd not be here. Note the good parts of past attempts and the one missing piece.
   - **S**ell the vacation (destination, not the flight): under ~3 minutes. Give about three pillars of the offer, tied to what *they* said, with one story or metaphor. Skip feature lists.
   - **E**xplain away concerns: see objections.
   - **R**einforce after yes: stop selling, take the next step, restate their goal, set next steps and book the next touchpoint. Cuts refunds and regret.
4. **Surface obstacles BEFORE the price.** Obstacles come before the price, objections after it. Ask early: who else is involved in this decision? when would you start? how much time can you give this? "You may be wondering if this is a lot of money. Let's talk about it."
5. **State the price calmly, then stay quiet.** Anchor on the full price first; offer prepay or terms as a benefit. Test prices before calls, never discount live; change terms or scope instead.
6. **Ask for the sale. If yes, stop talking and take the next step.** If not, run the objection routine and ask again after each real concern is resolved.
7. **Book the next meeting from this meeting (BAMFAM)** with calendars open. "Let's circle back" with no date means the deal is leaking.

## 3A reframe (use right after any "not yes")
1. **Acknowledge**: repeat their point in your words ("So timing is on your mind.").
2. **Associate**: link it to something positive or to someone else: "that's what our most serious clients ask", or a past client story, or a named expert ("our lead told me this week...").
3. **Ask**: a question that moves forward ("What would need to be true for this to feel right?").
Use a third-party story (another customer, another prospect today) to say hard truths without insulting them. Ask permission before a blunt point: "Can I tell you what I told someone in your position?"

## Universal stall questions (in order)
"What's your main concern?" -> "What are you most afraid will happen?" -> "What are you weighing?" -> "What would make this a yes?" -> "What would make it a no?" -> then ask again.
All-purpose closes: the *reason* close (is this the reason you've stayed stuck?), 1-10 scale ("what would make it a 10?"), best case / worst case played out honestly, zoom out to the big picture, stack two or three angles on the same obstacle.

## Detail questions are traps
When asked "how many certifications / what days / which features?", answer with a question first ("Which are you looking for?"), find the reason behind the reason, then show how your approach reaches that same result. If pressed: "I can't answer ethically before we've looked at your situation."

## Discovery questions bank
Intent: what made you reach out today? what's the goal? why that? what changes when you hit it? Problem: how long? what has it cost? what if it continues five years? Past: what have you tried, how did it go, what did you like/dislike? Situation: where are you now / want to be / what's in between? who else decides? when would you start? Test: if this were perfect would you do it? where are you 1-10?

## Follow-up
- First follow-up is speed: contact new leads within minutes. Then repeated, personal, short touches over days.
- Ghosted: a light, human, short message ("Did I lose you?") beats a pitch. No-for-now: pulse check every 3 to 6 months. Always give one useful thing, one easy reply.
- After yes: first 48 hours matter; welcome, next steps, keep serving.

## Drill mode (role-play)
Play a realistic prospect for their real offer. Use one objection at a time. After each round give: what worked, one thing to fix (only one), and have them repeat the better version 3 times. Rotate: opening, price moment, "think about it", partner, timing.

## Ethics guardrails (always on)
Tell the truth. Use urgency only if real. No guilt or pressure on someone who is a poor fit; walk away when it's not right. Soften jokes about spouses/weight/money from the source; keep humour respectful. Do not use these lines to push vulnerable people into purchases they can't afford.
