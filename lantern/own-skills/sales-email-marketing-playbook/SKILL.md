# Email Marketing Playbook (to people who already know you)

Summarised in original words from two public videos (`references/sources.md`). Numbers quoted by the speaker (including the multi-million campaign) are unverified, and the captions garbled some figures, so use them only as direction. The user may be a beginner: plain language, one step at a time.

## Core ideas
- Email to a list is the cheapest follow-up: you already got the contact. You reach inboxes based on your past engagement, not an algorithm lottery.
- Every email does one of two jobs: raise the odds they buy later, or get them to buy now. Mostly give value; ask sometimes.
- **Reward every action**: the open (first line is worth it), the read (one usable idea), the click (it pays off), the reply. If emails are reliably good, people open the next one. One weak email can break the habit, so send nothing rather than something empty.
- Fix the **format**, vary the **content** (same shape each time, new idea inside).

## Consent and safety (always)
Only email people who opted in or are existing customers. Include your business name, address and an easy unsubscribe in every marketing email, and honour opt-outs fast (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, NDPA and similar vary by country). Easy unsubscribing also protects deliverability. Real deadlines and real scarcity only; no fake countdowns.

## The value newsletter (the "one-minute email")
1. **Subject**: a consistent series name + a short promise or topic (builds habit), or a proof-led line. Test either.
2. **Preview text**: always write it (curiosity, not a repeat of the subject).
3. **Opener**: a quote, top post or striking stat useful on its own.
4. **Body (about 200 words or fewer)**: one problem -> one fix -> one tactic they can use today, with a concrete example or number.
5. **CTA**: contextual to this email and to the reader type (beginner vs advanced); one or two links at most.
6. **P.S.**: always add one (bonus tip, second link, light meme). It's widely read.
Plain text style, mostly no images, mobile-first (preview on phone). Don't paste whole blog posts: give one nugget, link for the rest.

## Cadence and starting out
- Start with a realistic rhythm (e.g. monthly, then weekly). Batch-write a block of emails (e.g. 12) before the first send. A quality bar beats a fixed schedule.
- Segment by what you learned at sign-up (type of business, size, goal) and separate buyers from non-buyers. Relevance beats polish.
- Expect a one-time bump in unsubscribes the first time you email an old list; keep unsubscribing easy and accept it.

## Welcome email (after sign-up)
Deliver the promised freebie; ask for a quick reply ("reply 'yes' and I'll send it") because replies help inbox placement; remind them what they'll get as a subscriber; tease what's next. Template in `references/templates.md`.

## Timing
Mid-week mornings to early afternoons local time are a reasonable start (B2C often Mon/Tue, B2B often Wed). Treat this as a small edge and test on your own list.

## The 7-day challenge campaign (to wake a dormant list)
1. Take something that already works in your paid product and turn it into a small, complete fix for one narrow problem (free). Solving it should expose the next problem your paid offer solves.
2. Frame as a challenge: clear outcome, short fixed time (e.g. 7 days), simple daily steps, optional prizes/leaderboard/live sessions.
3. Invite buyers and non-buyers; test on **10-15%** (or less) first, fix what breaks, then expand.
4. Weave the paid offer into the last days with a real deadline; then objection, testimonial and last-call emails.
5. Review (send > open > click > register > attend > buy), change **one** thing, rerun with a new wrapper (name, landing page, season/life event). Roughly quarterly, with value-only emails in between.
Pay in value before you ask, so goodwill stays healthy.

## Metrics
Click-through rate first (open rate is unreliable after privacy changes): clicks/sent and clicks/opened. Also unsubscribes per send, replies, placement in Promotions, revenue per email sent. Judge over weeks, not per email.

## Mistakes to avoid
Ignoring the list after the first sale; blasting the whole list with an untested promo; sending filler to hit a schedule; images/heavy HTML/many links; blank preview text; same CTA wording every time; no reason to stay subscribed after the welcome; treating open rate as the main metric.

## What to produce
Ask what they sell, who's on the list and how big, and their goal. Then deliver: an email calendar, newsletter drafts in the one-minute format (with subject, preview, P.S.), a welcome email, a challenge plan with the full email sequence, or a metrics review. Use `references/templates.md`.
