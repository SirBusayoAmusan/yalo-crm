# The Lantern Hours: Prompt Pack

Every prompt from the book as plain text. Open the Code tab in the Claude desktop app, choose your Lantern-Work folder, paste a prompt, replace anything in [CAPITALS] with your own details, and press Enter.


## Before You Begin: Lighting the Lamp

### A safe first test

```
Please create a file called hello.txt in this folder. Inside it, write today's date and one sentence saying what this folder is for: my work with the book The Lantern Hours. Then tell me the full path of the file you made.
```

### Check the skills are visible

```
Look at the skills you have available. List five whose names begin with founder- and five whose names begin with ig-, with one plain sentence each on what they do. Do not run any of them yet. Finish by telling me roughly how many skills you can see in total.
```

```

## Chapter 8: The Conversation That Closes

### Prepare for a sales call

```
/sales-closing-playbook Pre-call prep, please. Tomorrow I am meeting [NAME], who runs [BUSINESS] in [PLACE]. They replied to my message saying: [PASTE WHAT THEY SAID]. I sell [OFFER] for [PRICE]. Make me a one-page prep card: what I know, one specific thing to mention, a 20-second opening agenda, five discovery questions, the obstacles I should raise before the price, how to state the price, and the next step to book before we finish.
```

### Drill: I need to think about it

```
/sales-closing-playbook Drill mode. Play the owner of [THEIR BUSINESS], who likes my offer but says "I need to think about it". My offer: [OFFER AND PRICE]. Stay in character and only reveal the real concern if I ask good questions. After each of my answers, step out of character, tell me what worked and the one thing to fix, and give me the better line to repeat three times.
```

### Drill: the price

```
/sales-closing-playbook Drill mode, price objections. You are [TYPE OF BUYER] considering [OFFER] at [PRICE]. Start with "That's more than I expected", then try "I can get something cheaper from [ALTERNATIVE]". Respond realistically to what I say. After each round, give me one fix only, and remind me never to discount the price; suggest changes to terms or a smaller first step instead.
```

### Drill: the partner or boss

```
/sales-closing-playbook Drill mode. Play [NAME], who runs [BUSINESS] with a business partner and says "I'll need to run it past my partner first". My offer: [OFFER AND PRICE]. Let me practise asking what the partner might object to, and whether they need approval or support. Keep it respectful and realistic. Score each round, give one fix, and the better line to repeat three times.
```

### Drill: the timing

```
/sales-closing-playbook Drill mode, timing objections. You are a busy [TYPE OF BUYER] who says "Maybe after the holidays, we're flat out right now". My offer: [OFFER AND PRICE]. Let me practise separating time from priority without being pushy. If I truly cannot help them now, let me practise booking a specific date to talk again. One fix per round.
```

### Drill: not sure, burned before

```
/sales-closing-playbook Drill mode. Play [NAME] of [BUSINESS], who says "We tried stocking [SIMILAR THING] before and it didn't sell". My offer: [OFFER AND PRICE]. Let me practise asking what happened last time and what would need to be different. If I start arguing or over-explaining, stop me. After each round give one fix and the better line to repeat three times.
```

### Answer an objection by email

```
/sales-sellbird-playbooks Use the objection handling playbook. A prospect replied to my email: [PASTE THEIR REPLY]. My offer: [OFFER]. First tell me which kind of reply this is: brush-off, condition, smokescreen or genuine objection. Then draft one short, honest reply that ends with a single easy question.
